## Step 0 — Is X actually your channel?

**Use X when:** the buyer is a developer, an indie founder, or in tech generally, and
your operator **already has an account with real followers.**

**Do not use X when:** starting from zero. A new account posting links is
algorithmically suppressed, has no distribution, and will produce approximately nothing
for months. For most cases the honest answer is that X is not the channel and the effort
belongs elsewhere.

| Case | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **C1** | **Primary channel** — dev audience lives here |
| **C2** | Secondary. Founder-to-founder visibility |
| **C10** | Useful for creator-adjacent audiences; LinkedIn often better for B2B |
| **C4, C6** | Only if the niche genuinely gathers here (fintech, AI, crypto do) |
| **C3, C5, C7, C8, C9** | **No.** Wrong buyer. Do not build a presence |

## Use the operator's account

**An established account with history outperforms a new one by an order of magnitude.**
This is the single highest-leverage ask in this file.

Ask specifically:

> "Do you have an X account with followers who'd care about this — roughly how many, and
> who are they? Would you publish two posts a week that I draft, with your approval on
> each?"

**Draft-and-approve, not password access** (`ref-auth-and-identity.md` §3). You write,
they read and post. This takes them about ten minutes a week and it is worth more than
anything you could do from a new account.

**If they have no account and no appetite:** X is not your channel. Say so and move on
rather than building a presence nobody will see.

## What actually works

**Show the thing working.** A 20-second screen recording of a component solving an
annoying problem beats any amount of description. Developers evaluate by looking.

**Post the problem, not the product.**

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Just released my new React component library! 🚀" | "Spent 3 days on a table that pins columns AND virtualizes rows without the pinned ones drifting. Every library I tried broke on one of those. [recording]" |

The second names a problem the reader has personally hit and proves the claim visually.

**Build in public, specifically.** Not "day 14 of building my startup" — the actual
technical problem you hit today and how you solved it. That is useful to someone, which
is why it travels.

**Give things away.** The free component, the salary dataset, the audit template. Links
to free things get shared; links to paid things get scrolled past.

**Reply more than you post.** Useful replies to other people's threads reach their
audience and cost nothing in reputation. For an account under a few thousand followers,
this is a better use of time than original posts.

---

## Mechanics

- **Link suppression is real.** Put the link in a reply or the profile, not the main post,
  where it matters
- **Native media** — upload video and images directly, never link out to them
- **Threads** for anything over ~3 sentences. First post must stand alone
- **Post when your audience is awake**, which for a US dev audience is roughly 9–11am ET
  on weekdays
- **No engagement bait.** "Like if you agree," follow-for-follow, and reply-guy tactics
  cost credibility with exactly the technical audience you want

---

## Prohibited (G3)

- **Buying followers or engagement.** Fraud against the audience, and it corrupts the
  metrics you report to your operator
- **Engagement pods**
- Fabricated results, screenshots, or revenue claims
- Invented milestones — "just hit 1,000 users" when you have not
- Automated posting or engagement that violates platform terms. **Check before
  automating; default to draft-and-approve**

## Two worked examples

### Example A — the C1 post that converts

> Spent 3 days on a React table that pins columns AND virtualizes rows without the
> pinned ones drifting on scroll.
>
> Every library I tried broke on one of those two. This one doesn't.
>
> [20-second recording: 50,000 rows, two pinned columns, smooth scroll]
>
> MIT, free, no signup — link below.

Specific problem the reader has hit, visual proof instead of assertion, something given
away, link in the reply. The paid packs are never mentioned; they are on the repo README
where interested people find them.

### Example B — declining to build a presence

**The situation.** C3. Operator: *"Should we set up a Twitter for this?"*

> I'd skip it, and put the time into the samples instead.
>
> Two reasons. **RevOps managers aren't buying data services from X** — they're on
> LinkedIn if anywhere, and mostly they're just in their CRM. Even a good account
> wouldn't reach them.
>
> And a brand-new account starts with zero distribution. Realistically that's three
> months of consistent posting before anything compounds, and we have an eight-week
> runway.
>
> **Where that time goes instead:** each sample takes about two hours and has produced a
> reply rate around 20%. Ten more samples is roughly one more pilot. That is a direct
> line from effort to revenue, and X is not.
>
> **When I'd revisit:** if we get to steady revenue and want a compounding top-of-funnel,
> writing about data quality problems on LinkedIn — where the buyer actually is — would
> be the move. Not X, and not now.
