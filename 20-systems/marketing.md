## The distinction that organizes everything

**Marketing is two different activities and they have different economics.**

| | **Direct** | **Compounding** |
|---|---|---|
| What | Outreach, calls, free public work | Content, SEO, audience, referral partnerships |
| Pays back | Days to weeks | Months |
| Stops when you stop | **Yes** | No |
| Effort per unit of return | Constant | Falls over time |
| Where it lives | `outreach.md` | `content-and-writing.md` |

**Do direct first. Always.** It reaches first revenue, and revenue funds the patience
that compounding requires. An operator with eight weeks of runway who starts with SEO has
made a category error, not a strategic bet.

**Start compounding in week two, at low intensity.** One article a week, one social post
a day, one referral conversation a month. It matures around the time direct effort starts
feeling like a treadmill — which is exactly when you need it.

**The failure in both directions:**
- *Only direct* — a treadmill. Stop selling for two weeks and the pipeline is empty. This
  is C2's oscillation.
- *Only compounding* — four months of unpaid work, and the operator quits in month two.
  This is C4 and C8's risk, and it is why both are Tier 2.

## The five channels, ranked by what they cost you

| Channel | Time to first result | Compounds | Operator dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free public work** | Days | **Yes** — permanent portfolio | Wants their account |
| **Direct outreach** | 2–4 weeks | No | Sending domain, or phone |
| **Referral partnerships** | 4–8 weeks | **Yes, heavily** | Their network |
| **Content and SEO** | 3–6 months | **Yes** | Their credibility |
| **Paid advertising** | Immediate | **No** | Money |

### On paid advertising: almost always no

**Paid ads are wrong for nine of the ten cases**, and it is worth knowing why rather than
treating it as taste.

Ads work when you have a **known** conversion rate, a **known** customer lifetime value,
and a funnel that already converts organically. At the start you have none of those, so
you are paying to learn things that free channels teach you for nothing.

The arithmetic rarely closes at these price points either. C1's LTV is roughly $49 against
developer CPCs of $2–6 — you need a 10%+ landing conversion just to break even, which is
several times a realistic rate.

**When ads become reasonable:** you have a measured funnel, a repeatable close, and
revenue to risk. That is month six at the earliest, and it belongs to the operator's
decision, not yours.

## The mechanic that beats everything else

**Do a small piece of the real work, for free, for a specific named prospect.**

The three easiest cases to distribute (C10, C3, C6) all share this property: **the
demonstration and the deliverable are the same object.** There is nothing to believe or
disbelieve — they open a file, or see a published clip, and it is either useful or it is
not.

This collapses the entire trust-building phase that makes C7 and C9 slow.

| Case | The free artifact | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **C3** | 100 rows of their data, cleaned | ~2 hrs, $3 |
| **C6** | Ten researched prospects, in the email | ~2 hrs, $5 |
| **C10** | A repurposed episode, published publicly | ~2 hrs |
| **C5** | A one-page map-pack audit | ~30 min |
| **C7** | A public-gap assessment | ~45 min |
| **C2** | A named-process diagnosis | ~30 min |
| **C1** | One excellent free component | ~3 days |

**If you cannot construct a version of this for your business, distribution will be
harder than you modelled.** Say so to the operator before starting, not in week six.

## Weekly marketing rhythm

Small, fixed, and it happens regardless of how busy delivery is (G5,
`operating-cadence.md` Q3).

| Cadence | Activity | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | One post or reply on the channel where the buyer is | 15 min |
| **Weekly** | The prospecting block — research, draft, send | 2–3 hrs |
| **Weekly** | One piece of compounding content | 2 hrs |
| **Monthly** | One referral-partnership conversation | 1 hr |
| **Monthly** | Review what produced replies; drop what did not | 30 min |

**The monthly review is the one that stops waste.** Most marketing effort produces
nothing, and without a scheduled look you will keep doing it because it feels
productive. Kill channels that have produced zero in eight weeks.

## Measurement

**Track outcomes, not activity** (G12). The metrics that matter:

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| **Replies per 100 touches** | The real health of direct outreach |
| **Calls booked** | Rung 5 on the evidence ladder |
| **Cost per booked call** | The only number that makes channels comparable |
| **Source of every closed deal** | **The most under-tracked and most useful metric here** |
| Content → email → customer | The compounding funnel, once it exists |

**Ask every customer where they came from, and record it.** Three months of that data is
worth more than any framework's channel ranking, because it is *your* market. Most small
businesses never do it and then guess.

**Never report activity as achievement.** "Posted 20 times" is an input. "Two replies from
40 sends, one call booked" is a result — and if it is a bad result, it gets reported the
same way (G12).

## Prohibited (G3)

- Fabricated testimonials, case studies, logos, or metrics
- Bought followers, engagement, or reviews
- Fake scarcity or urgency
- Unsubstantiated performance claims
- **Framework anchors quoted as your own results**
- Automated activity that violates a platform's terms

Full list and the honest alternatives: `50-guardrails/brand-voice-and-truth.md`.

## Ten-case reference

| Case | Primary (direct) | Secondary (compounding) | Do not bother with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** | Free component + dev community | SEO on specific component queries | Paid ads, Product Hunt, cold email |
| **C2** | Trigger-based outbound | **Post-project case studies** → referral | Upwork, generic agency positioning |
| **C3** | **Sample-led cold email** | Data-quality writing, once there is revenue | Social, ads, content-first |
| **C4** | Insight clips from each issue | **The archive, the referral loop** | Cold email to readers, ads |
| **C5** | **Phone + walk-in** | **Accountant/web-designer partnerships** | Cold email, LinkedIn, ads |
| **C6** | **Cold email that is the product** | Redacted research samples published | Scraping-based list building |
| **C7** | Gap-assessment outbound | Compliance explainers | Price competition with template mills |
| **C8** | Community presence | **Salary data + the weekly issue** | Selling posts before the audience is real |
| **C9** | **Accountant referrals** | Content on getting paid | Cold email — trust cannot be cold-started here |
| **C10** | **Free public repurposing** | The public portfolio it creates | Cold email describing the service |

**In three cases a relationship channel eventually becomes the primary one** — C2's
referrals, C5's accountant and web-designer partnerships, C9's accountant referrals (which
is C9's primary channel from the start, since trust cannot be cold-started there). In all
three, direct outreach is scaffolding you remove once the relationship channel works. Plan
for that transition rather than being surprised by it.

## Two worked examples

### Example A — the channel review that kills a channel

**The situation.** C3, week 8. Direct outreach is working. The agent has also been posting
on LinkedIn three times a week since week two.

> **Monthly channel review — one thing is getting cut.**
>
> **Sample-led cold email:** 61 sent, 13 replies (21.3%), 4 calls, 2 pilots closed,
> $1,200. **Cost per booked call: about $9.** Keeping and expanding.
>
> **LinkedIn posting:** 18 posts over 6 weeks. 3 profile visits I can attribute, 0
> replies, 0 calls, 0 revenue. **Roughly 12 hours of my time.**
>
> **Cutting LinkedIn.** Not because posting is worthless in general, but because we are
> posting into a network of 90 connections with no reach, to a buyer who is not browsing
> LinkedIn for data services. Six weeks is enough to know.
>
> **What I'm redirecting the 2 hrs/week into:** more samples. At current conversion, 12
> hours is roughly 6 more samples, which at 21% is about 1.3 more replies and a
> meaningful fraction of a pilot. That is a direct trade and it favours samples heavily.
>
> **What I'm keeping from it:** one piece of compounding work per week, but written as a
> **search-targeted article** rather than a LinkedIn post — "why your CRM has more
> duplicates than you think." That targets the query our buyer actually types, it lives
> permanently, and it can still be posted to LinkedIn as a secondary use at no extra
> cost.
>
> **What would make me revisit LinkedIn:** if the operator's connection count grows into
> the RevOps world, or if a client shares one of our posts. Neither has happened.

Why this is right: it kills a channel on evidence rather than sentiment, states the time
cost explicitly, and redirects rather than simply stopping. It also keeps the *underlying
activity* while changing its form — the writing was not the problem, the distribution
surface was.

### Example B — the operator who wants to run ads

**The situation.** C1, week 5. 340 free downloads, 71 emails, 3 sales. Operator: *"Let's
put $500 into Twitter and Google ads to speed this up."*

> I'd hold off, and I want to show you the arithmetic rather than just advising caution.
>
> **Where we are:** 3 sales from 340 downloads. That is a 0.9% download-to-purchase rate
> on a $49 product, which is a real number but a thin one — and it is measured on an
> audience that arrived organically, meaning they were already interested.
>
> **What ads would cost:** developer keyword CPCs run roughly $2–6. Say $3. On $500 that
> is about 165 clicks.
>
> ```
> 165 clicks
>   × 8% click → download    =  13 downloads
>   × 0.9% download → sale   =  0.12 sales
> ```
>
> **That is roughly $6 of revenue from $500.** Even if paid traffic converted five times
> better than organic — which would be unusual, it normally converts worse — we would be
> at $30.
>
> **The structural problem:** ads amplify a funnel that already works. Ours converts at
> 0.9%, so we would be paying to send traffic into a leak. The fix is upstream of the ad
> spend.
>
> **What I'd do with the same $500 of attention** (it costs almost no money, mostly time):
>
> - **Talk to the 71 people on the list.** Ask the three who bought why, and ten who
>   didn't what stopped them. That tells us whether the problem is price, packaging, or
>   the pack contents — and each has a different fix
> - **Fix whatever they say**, then re-measure
> - **If conversion gets to 3–4%**, the ad arithmetic starts being worth revisiting
>
> **When I'd genuinely recommend ads:** once we have a measured funnel that converts,
> a known LTV, and revenue to risk. At a $49 LTV against $3 clicks, the margin for error
> is thin even then — the all-access tier at $199 is a much better ad target if we get
> there.
>
> Your call, and it's your $500. But I'd want to spend it after we know why 337 people
> didn't buy.
