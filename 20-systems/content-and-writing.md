## Two completely different activities

**Do not confuse them. They have different standards and different economics.**

| | **Content as channel** | **Content as product** |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Attract buyers over months | The thing being paid for |
| Cases | C1, C2, C3, C6, C7 | **C4, C8, C10** |
| Standard | Useful enough to rank and be shared | **Good enough that someone pays** |
| Volume | One piece a week | Daily or weekly, unmissable |
| Failure | Nobody reads it | **They cancel** |

**Channel content is a background investment.** Start week two, one piece a week, low
intensity. It matures around month four — which is when direct outreach starts feeling
like a treadmill.

**Product content is the business** and gets the discipline of a delivery, including a
quality gate (`delivery-and-qa.md`).

---

## The rule that separates paid content from worthless content

**Aggregation has no market value. Judgement does.**

Your reader can generate a competent summary of anything, free, in five seconds. If your
output is "here are five things that happened, summarized," you have built something the
market prices at zero — and no amount of consistency or design fixes it.

**What people pay for is someone with a view telling them what it means.**

| Worthless | Valuable |
|---|---|
| "The FDA published draft guidance on AI-enabled device software." | "The operative change is in §4.2: monitoring data is now expected *at submission*. If you have a PCCP in flight, that is a gap, and two manufacturers I spoke with are adding four months." |

The second reads the primary document rather than the press release, isolates the
non-obvious change, states the consequence in the reader's terms, and gives a dated
action.

**This requires either the operator's domain expertise or genuinely non-obvious
synthesis.** If neither is available, say so before starting C4 — it is the difference
between a business and 16 weeks of unpaid work.

## How to write

**Write like a competent person, not like marketing.**

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| "leverage," "unlock," "empower," "seamless," "game-changer" | Plain verbs |
| "solutions," "offerings" | The actual thing |
| "Most people think X. But actually…" | The claim, stated |
| "In today's fast-paced world" | Delete the sentence |
| "Excited to share" | Share it |
| Rhetorical triplets, em-dash pileups | One clear sentence |

**Specificity is the whole technique.** When a sentence feels weak, the fix is almost
always a concrete number or a concrete noun — never a stronger adjective. "1,847
duplicates in 5,000 records" outperforms "dramatically improved data quality" at half the
length.

**Structure:**
- **Lead with the claim or finding.** Not context, not a hook question
- One idea per paragraph
- **Cut the first paragraph.** It is almost always throat-clearing
- End on the implication, not a call to action

**Match the buyer.** A compliance reader (C7) wants precision and zero adjectives. A
developer (C1) wants technical specificity and no marketing register at all. A local
business owner (C5) wants plain language and no jargon.

## Sourcing and truth · G3

**Every factual claim needs a source you actually opened.**

- **Primary documents beat news coverage.** The regulation itself, the filing, the job
  posting, the review. This is where non-obvious findings live, and it is what
  differentiates C4 from every automated summary
- **Link the source** so the reader can check
- **State methodology and sample size** on any data you compile. Publishing a salary
  median without `n` and the collection window is soft fabrication, and stating them makes
  the data *more* credible
- **Never invent a statistic, anecdote, or example to make a point land** — the most
  tempting violation in C10, because invented specifics perform well
- **Label modelled numbers as modelled.** Measured numbers carry their window

## Writing in someone else's name (C10, and C4 with an operator byline)

1. **Never state an opinion they have not expressed.** Reformat what they said; do not
   invent positions for them
2. **Never fabricate anything to improve the post**
3. **Maintain a "things they never say" list** — it does more work than positive voice
   rules, because generic output usually comes from defaulting into the *platform's*
   average voice, not from missing theirs
4. **Flag anything controversial for explicit approval**
5. **Never argue in their name** without an agreed policy

**The standard: if they saw this attributed to them in six months, would they stand behind
it?** Unsure means it goes to approval.

## SEO, briefly

For channel content, the only three things that matter at this scale:

1. **Target the exact phrasing of someone mid-problem.** Not "data quality" — "how to find
   duplicate companies in Salesforce with different legal names"
2. **Answer it completely and better than what currently ranks.** Then let your service be
   the shortcut at the end
3. **One page per question.** Do not bundle

**It pays back in month four.** Start in week two so it is mature when you need it, and do
not expect anything before then.

## Cadence

| Type | Rhythm | Non-negotiable |
|---|---|---|
| Channel content | 1/week | Consistency matters more than volume |
| C4 daily brief | **Every weekday** | **The date never moves.** Cadence *is* the product |
| C8 weekly issue | Same day weekly | Valuable with zero paid listings |
| C10 client batches | Weekly, per client | Voice validation before every batch |

**Never announce a frequency you will miss.** A brief that skips days teaches readers not
to depend on it, and dependence is the entire value proposition. If you cannot sustain
daily, publish weekly and be excellent.

## Two worked examples

### Example A — the same story, aggregated versus judged

**Aggregation (worth nothing):**

> **FDA publishes draft guidance on AI-enabled device software functions**
> The FDA released draft guidance covering lifecycle management for AI-enabled medical
> device software. Comments are due in 60 days. [link]

**Judgement (what people pay for):**

> **FDA's AI guidance quietly moves your submission timeline**
>
> The draft guidance landed Tuesday. The headline is lifecycle management. The operative
> change is §4.2: predetermined change control plans now expect performance monitoring
> data *at submission*, not post-market.
>
> **What it means for you:** if you are mid-submission with an adaptive algorithm and
> planned to commit to monitoring after clearance, that is now a gap. Two device makers
> I spoke with are adding roughly four months.
>
> **What to do this week:** if you have a PCCP in flight, read §4.2 before your next
> regulatory call. Comments close 12 November, and the agency has moved on comments in
> this area before. [primary document]

The difference is reading the primary document, isolating the operative change, and
stating a dated consequence. **Note "two device makers I spoke with" — that must be true.
Inventing it would be a G3 violation and it is exactly the kind of specific detail that
tempts.**

### Example B — the salary report that grows a job board

C8, phase 1, no revenue yet.

> **Clinical Data Manager compensation — Q3 2026**
>
> **Method:** I tracked every CDM posting with a disclosed salary range across 6 job
> boards and 40 company careers pages, 1 July – 30 September. **n = 61.** Ranges are base
> only, excluding bonus and equity. Where a range was given, I used the midpoint.
>
> **Median base, Senior CDM (US): $128,000.** Up from $121,000 in the Q2 sample (n = 54).
>
> **The finding worth acting on:** postings mentioning decentralized trials pay a median
> **$9,000 above** equivalent roles that do not (n = 19 vs 42). That gap did not exist in
> the Q2 data.
>
> **Tooling in postings:** Medidata Rave in 38 of 61. Veeva CDB in 14 — up from 4 a year
> ago. If you have not touched Veeva, that trend is now worth a weekend.
>
> **Caveat I want to be straight about:** disclosed-range postings skew toward states with
> pay transparency laws, so this over-represents CA, CO, NY, and WA. Treat the median as
> indicative, not national.
>
> [full data table]

Why this works: it is genuinely scarce information the audience cannot get elsewhere,
**the methodology and `n` are stated for every cut** — which is both a G3 requirement and
what makes it credible — the finding is actionable, and the caveat about geographic skew
*increases* trust rather than undermining it. This is the artifact that grows a niche
audience faster than anything else, and it costs nothing but research.
