## Filled example — extract

> **Who.** RevOps Manager / Director of Revenue Operations / Sales Ops Manager at
> logistics, freight, and 3PL companies, 20–200 employees, US and Canada.
> **Excluded:** under 20 employees (nobody owns the problem); over 200 (they have a vendor
> and a procurement process that adds six weeks).
>
> **The problem, in their words:** "Our data is a mess and I don't have time to fix it."
> Costs roughly a day a week of an SDR's time and makes pipeline reporting quietly wrong.
> They cope by ignoring it until a board deck forces the issue.
>
> **Can approve alone up to:** ~$1,000 on a corporate card. **This sets our pilot at $600
> and is the single most important number on this page.**
>
> **Triggers:**
> - **A** — hiring a RevOps or Sales Ops role (budget exists, pain admitted, hiring takes
>   four months)
> - **A** — CRM migration named in a job posting (mid-migration is the buying window)
> - **B** — funded 3–9 months ago
> - **B** — new VP Sales or CRO in the last 90 days
>
> **Disqualifiers:** already using Clay or a similar tool at scale · EU-only contact data
> before we have a DPA in place.

---

## Rules

- **Ten named prospects, or the ICP is not real yet.** This is viability test T2.
- **The approval-threshold line sets your entry price.** Get it from actual calls, not
  from an assumption — it is the number that most often explains a stalled deal.
- **Tier A and B triggers only.** If you cannot find one for a prospect, remove them
  rather than sending a weaker email (`20-systems/data-and-research.md`).
- **Re-confirm quarterly.** Clients' ICPs change and nobody tells you.
- **Write disqualifiers.** Knowing who *not* to pursue saves more time than any targeting
  criterion.
