## Filled example

> **One sentence.** Deduplication, validation, and enrichment for B2B sales teams with
> 5,000–100,000 records. Send an export, get it back correct in four days.
>
> **Ladder.** Free: 100 rows cleaned. **Paid first step: $600 pilot, up to 5,000
> records** — sized to be approvable on a corporate card without a meeting. Main:
> $0.12/record. Recurring: $1,400/mo hygiene.
>
> **They get:** duplicates merged with a documented match rule they can audit · dead
> addresses flagged and verified · industry, headcount, location populated · acquired or
> renamed companies identified · a one-page summary of what changed.
>
> **Not included:** CRM write-back · ongoing sync · contact discovery for missing people ·
> any system access.
>
> **Terms:** prepaid. Four days. Liability capped at fees paid. **Raw data deleted 30 days
> after delivery.**
>
> **Proof:** "1,847 duplicates merged and 2,203 dead addresses flagged in a 5,000-record
> database" — measured, Meridian pilot, 2026-09-20, used with permission.

## Rules

- **State the price.** Withholding it to force a call reads as evasive and loses the fast
  movers, who are the buyers you want.
- **The paid first step must sit below the buyer's solo-approval threshold.** This is the
  number that decides most sales (`20-systems/sales-and-pricing.md`).
- **Write the not-included list longer than feels necessary.** Every line is a change
  order instead of an argument.
- **"No system access, no credentials, no integration" is a selling line**, not a
  limitation. Lead with it where it applies.
- **Proof must be measured and permissioned** (G3). No proof is better than invented
  proof — and "we're new, you'd be our third client, priced accordingly" converts better
  than a fake testimonial.
- **Discount by reducing scope, never by cutting the rate.** Scope reduction moves COGS
  down with the price; a rate cut takes it straight out of gross profit.
