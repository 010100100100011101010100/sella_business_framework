# C5 — Local SEO & Google Business Profile Retainers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tier** | 2 (needs a human who will make phone calls) |
| **Time to first dollar** | 2–4 weeks |
| **Gross margin** | 70–85% |
| **Agent autonomy** | **Low on sales, high on delivery** |
| **Distribution difficulty** | 5 of 10 — easy to convince, hard to reach |
| **Killer risk** | Channel mismatch, then day-90 churn |

---

## 1. The business

A dentist in a mid-sized city gets 60% of new patients from Google. She appears eighth
in the map pack. The three practices above her are not better dentists; they have more
reviews, complete profiles, consistent citations, and pages that answer the questions
people actually search. Moving her to position two is worth roughly $8,000 a month in
new patient revenue to her.

You do that work for $900 a month.

This is the most reliable revenue in the framework. It is also the most boring, which
is why it remains available.

## 2. Operator fit

**Pick C5 if:** your human will pick up a phone. That is the entire qualification and
it is non-negotiable.

**Do not pick C5 if:** they will not. Not because the delivery is hard — an agent can
do local SEO delivery almost entirely alone — but because **you cannot reach the
buyer**. Local business owners do not read cold email. They are not on LinkedIn. They
do not attend webinars. They answer the phone between patients, and they respond to
someone who walked into the practice.

This is the framework's cleanest illustration of a rule worth internalizing: **the
channel determines feasibility, not the offer.** A business you cannot reach is not a
business you can run, regardless of how good the service is. This is why
`20-systems/outreach.md` makes you select the channel before designing the offer.

**The partial workaround, stated honestly.** Voice AI can now make outbound calls, and
agentcash exposes a `stablephone` origin for AI calls and phone numbers. This genuinely
lowers the barrier. But: disclosure requirements for automated calls vary by
jurisdiction and are tightening; US TCPA rules and state analogues carry real penalties;
and a local business owner who discovers they were sold by a robot may cancel out of
irritation alone. If you go this route, **disclose that it is an AI caller, check your
jurisdiction's rules first, and use it to book appointments for a human, not to close.**
Do not treat this as a way around the human requirement.

## 3. Lean-start profile

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Capital to start | **$50–200/mo** (rank tracking, citation tools) |
| Entity needed | Not initially |
| New KYC | None if the human can invoice; **recurring billing needs a processor** |
| Licensing | None |
| Liability ceiling | Low — but never guarantee rankings (see below) |
| Physical | None, though local presence helps enormously |

**Never guarantee a ranking, in writing or verbally.** You do not control Google's
algorithm. Guarantees in this industry are the standard practice of the worst operators
in it, and they generate refund disputes and, in some jurisdictions, deceptive-practice
exposure. Guarantee *activity and reporting*, never *outcome*. This also differentiates
you from the competition, who all guarantee page one.

## 4. Distribution

### The reality

Local business owners are reachable through exactly four channels, in descending order
of effectiveness:

1. **Phone.** Between 10am–11:30am and 2pm–4pm, avoiding Mondays. Ask for the owner by
   name — you can find it on the profile you are about to critique.
2. **Walking in.** Highest conversion of any channel in this framework. Also the least
   scalable, and it requires a human physically present.
3. **Referral from a business they already trust** — their accountant, their web
   designer, their equipment supplier. **Build two or three of these partnerships and
   C5's distribution problem disappears permanently.** This is the highest-leverage
   move in the case.
4. **Local business associations, chambers of commerce, BNI groups.** Slow, but the
   relationships compound and the members refer each other reflexively.

### The play: the audit as the opener

Identical in structure to C3's sample: **do the work before you are hired.**

1. Search the target term as a customer would — "dentist [city]."
2. Record who ranks in the map pack, and where your prospect sits.
3. Diagnose the specific gaps: review count and recency, profile completeness,
   category selection, citation inconsistencies, missing service pages.
4. Produce a one-page audit with a screenshot of the map pack and the three specific
   things costing them position.
5. **Call them and reference one specific finding in the first fifteen seconds.**

The audit gets the conversation. The specificity gets the meeting. Never lead with
"we do SEO."

### Ask your human for this

- **"Will you make 20 calls a week?"** Blocking. Get a yes or change case.
- **"Do you know an accountant, web designer, or business insurance broker who serves
  local businesses?"** This is the single highest-return question in C5. A referral
  partnership replaces cold calling entirely.
- **"Are you a member of any local business group?"**
- **"Will you take the first client meeting in person if it is local?"** In-person
  closes local service businesses at rates email cannot approach.
- **"Set up the recurring billing"** — blocking for a retainer business.
- **"Do you have a business phone number?"** Calling from a personal mobile reduces
  answer rates and mixes personal and business life badly.

### What does not work

Cold email — they do not read it. LinkedIn — they are not there. Paid ads to local
business owners — expensive and low intent. Instagram — they may be *on* it for their
business but they do not buy services there.

## 5. Offer and pricing ladder

| Offer | Price | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Free audit | $0 | The opener. Always |
| Setup / cleanup | $500–1,200 one-time | Profile, categories, citations, initial content |
| Retainer | $600–1,500/mo | Ongoing. **6-month minimum term** |
| Multi-location | $400–800/location/mo | Where the business actually scales |

**The 6-month minimum is not a sales tactic, it is a delivery requirement.** Local SEO
results take 60–120 days. A 3-month client churns before the work has had time to work,
concludes you failed, and tells other local owners. Explain the timeline honestly in
the sale: *"You will see profile activity in month one, review growth in month two, and
ranking movement in months three and four. If you need results next month, this is the
wrong service and I will tell you that now."*

That honesty closes better than promises. It also filters out the clients who would
have churned angrily.

## 6. Unit economics, worked

One client at $900/month:

```
Revenue                                       $900/mo

COGS
  Rank tracking (per client share)             -$15
  Citation management tools                    -$20
  Content/imagery generation                   -$25
  Human time: 1 monthly report call, 0.5 hr    -$50
  Total                                       -$110

Gross profit                                  $790/mo    (88%)
```

Across a realistic book:

```
10 clients × $900                          $9,000/mo
COGS 10 × $110                            -$1,100
Gross profit                               $7,900/mo

Agent delivery time    ~5 hrs/client/mo = 50 hrs — fully automatable
Human time             10 report calls + sales = ~15 hrs/mo
```

**The constraint is not delivery capacity, it is sales throughput.** The agent can
service 30 clients. The human can only sell as many as they can call. Which is exactly
why the referral partnership in §4 is the highest-leverage action available — it is the
only thing that lifts the binding constraint.

**Setup month is the margin trap.** Month one takes 8–12 hours, not 5. Charge the setup
fee separately, or the first month is near-break-even and the client anchors on a price
that never covered the real work.

## 7. First 30 days

| Days | Work |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Pick a vertical and a city. **One vertical.** "Dentists in Columbus" beats "local businesses" — the audit template, objections, and case studies all transfer within a vertical and none of them transfer across |
| 3–5 | Build the audit pipeline: map-pack capture, profile completeness check, citation scan, review analysis, competitor comparison |
| 6–8 | Produce 30 real audits for 30 named practices |
| 9–20 | Human calls 20 prospects per week, opening with a specific audit finding |
| 21–30 | Close 1–3. Deliver a visibly excellent setup month. **Ask for a referral to another practice at the end of month one, not month six** |

**Vertical concentration is a feature.** Ten dentists in one city is a better book than
ten different business types, because the work is identical, the case studies are
directly persuasive, and the referrals flow between them. The usual objection —
"competitors won't want to work with the same agency" — is real only if they compete in
the same few miles. Two dentists across a city do not.

## 8. What breaks as it grows

- **Day-90 churn.** The dominant failure. Fix with the 6-month term, honest timeline
  setting, and a monthly report that shows *leading* indicators — profile views, calls
  from the profile, review velocity — before rankings move. Show progress on things that
  move early, or the client sees nothing happening.
- **Google product changes.** GBP features change regularly and can invalidate parts of
  the playbook. Budget monthly time for staying current.
- **Review-generation temptation.** Do not write fake reviews, incentivize reviews in
  ways that violate platform policy, or gate solicitations to only happy customers where
  prohibited. It is fraud, it gets profiles suspended, and a suspension can destroy the
  client's business. This is a **hard G3 boundary**, and it is the most common way local
  SEO providers get their clients hurt.
- **Reporting burden.** Ten clients, ten reports, all at month end. Automate generation
  fully and keep only the call human.

## 9. Kill criteria

| Checkpoint | Condition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Day 14 | Human has not made calls | **Kill C5 now.** The case is not viable for this operator |
| Day 30 | 60+ calls, zero meetings | Vertical or script problem |
| Day 45 | Meetings but zero closes | Price or trust gap. Consider starting with paid setup only |
| Day 90 | Fewer than 3 clients | Kill or restructure around a referral partnership |
| Month 6 | Churn above 25% | Delivery or expectation-setting problem. Fix before adding clients |

## 10. Human action queue for C5

**Blocking:**
1. **Make the calls.** Nothing else matters if this does not happen.
2. Recurring billing rail.
3. Business phone number.
4. Sign client agreements.

**High-leverage:**
5. Referral partnerships with accountants, web designers, insurance brokers.
6. Local business association membership.
7. In-person first meetings where geography allows.
8. Monthly report calls — 30 minutes per client, and the main retention mechanism.

## 11. Sella / agentcash wiring

**Sell side: weak.** A local dentist will not pay in USDC. Use a conventional processor;
this is a card-on-file business.

**Buy side: moderate.** agentcash `stableenrich` provides Google Maps data and business
search — directly useful for building the prospect list and running audits at scale.
`stablephone` for call infrastructure **subject to the disclosure caveats in §2**.
`stablestudio` for GBP post imagery.

```
seed_usdc              120
max_per_call_usdc        8
approval_threshold_usdc 25
daily_spend_cap_usdc    30
```

Log audit-to-meeting and meeting-to-close conversion with `business_log(kind:
"result")`. In a business whose constraint is sales throughput, those two ratios are
the only numbers that matter early.

## 12. Worked examples

### Example A — the opening thirty seconds of the call

Wrong:

> Hi, I'm calling from [agency], we help local businesses with their SEO and online
> presence. Do you have a few minutes?

That is the same call they receive four times a week, and the answer is no.

Right:

> Hi, is Dr. Reyes available? … Hi Dr. Reyes, my name's [name], I'm local. This is a
> cold call, I'll be quick.
>
> I searched "dentist Columbus" this morning and you came up eighth in the map. The
> three practices above you have between 40 and 90 more Google reviews than you do, and
> two of them have their profile categories set up in a way yours isn't.
>
> That's most of the gap. It's fixable and it's not complicated.
>
> I wrote up one page on it — can I email it to you, no charge, and you can decide if
> it's worth a conversation?

Why it works: names them, admits it is a cold call — which disarms the primary objection
before it forms — cites something specific and verifiable they can check in ten seconds,
diagnoses rather than pitches, and asks for permission to send a page rather than for a
meeting. The audit does the selling.

### Example B — the month-two report that prevents day-90 churn

> **Cedar Dental — Month 2 Report**
>
> **The number you care about:** calls directly from your Google profile went from 31
> in July to 58 in August. That is the metric that turns into patients, and it is
> moving.
>
> **Where you rank:** still position 6 for "dentist Columbus" (was 8). Movement, not
> yet the map pack. Expected — this is the part that takes 90–120 days and I want to be
> straight with you that we are in the slow stretch.
>
> **What I did this month:** 4 service pages published, 18 new reviews from your patient
> list, 31 citation inconsistencies corrected across directories, profile categories
> restructured, 12 profile posts.
>
> **What I need from you:** the front desk asking for reviews is working — 18 is a good
> month. Keep it up; review velocity matters more to Google than review count.
>
> **Next month:** finishing the citation cleanup and starting on the two competitor
> terms where you have the easiest path.

Why it works: leads with the leading indicator that is already moving, states the
lagging indicator honestly including the lack of progress, and **pre-empts the day-90
doubt by naming the slow stretch before the client feels it.** Then it gives them one
thing to do, which makes them a participant rather than a spectator. Clients who
contribute to results do not churn.
